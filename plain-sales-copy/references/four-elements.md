# Four elements

Every core unit contains these four, in this order. They are the structure of
an elevator pitch, a cold email, a hero block, a deck opener, and an outreach
note. Format changes; the four do not.

## 1. Pain and status quo

Hit what they did last night, or the hours they already spend. Do not hit a
distant vision.

- Client cares about their problem, not the product.
- Name the person and the work: "store managers spend 40 hours a month on
  roster and payroll by hand."
- Forbidden: "the future of X", "digital transformation", "unlock potential".

## 2. Cost of inaction

Loss aversion. Not "how good life is with us" — "what you lose this month
without a change".

- Convert the pain into a unit they already track: hours, dollars, missed
  orders, churned accounts, days of delay.
- May share a sentence with pain ("…that's $20k of manager time a month").
- If you cannot name a cost, you do not understand the pain yet. Ask or label
  an assumption. Do not skip the slot.

## 3. Mechanism and result

No feature lists. One mechanism, one measurable result.

Formula:

```
We [mechanism a grandmother can picture]
for [the person named in the pain]
so that [number + unit of result].
```

- Mechanism is a verb phrase they can watch: "connects the POS to staff LINE,
  one tap, roster and payroll in 5 seconds."
- Result is a number they can audit: "each store saves $20k of management
  cost a month."
- Proof sits here or as the next sentence: a named customer, a dated result.
  Unnamed "leading brands" is puff. If the name cannot be used, write
  `assumptions: proof unnamed` and use the number only.

## 4. Low-friction CTA

A request that is cheap to accept and awkward to refuse for no reason.

Allowed first asks:

- "If Wednesday afternoon is free, I send a 2-minute screen recording?"
- "Reply with last month's hours-on-roster and I'll run the same math."
- "I'll put a one-screen before/after next to your current spreadsheet?"

Forbidden first asks:

- "Let's sign."
- "Can I have an hour for a demo?"
- "Book a call on my calendar."
- "Start your free trial." (that's a close, not a pitch unit)

The CTA buys the next 15 minutes. The close lives in that next block, after
they asked to hear more.

## Sequence surfaces

On a landing page or deck, only the **first** unit needs all four. Later units
may be:

- more proof (named customer, number, time window)
- a closer look at the mechanism (still no puff, still grandma words)
- FAQ that answers "how?" and "how much?" — the success questions

Later units must not restart the pitch, add a vision paragraph, or introduce
a second product.
